# Critical evaluation: how far do random-split scores overstate real-world performance, and does AutoML protect against leakage and drift?

Generated by `scripts/run_critical_eval.py` (`src/critical_eval_writeup.py`) from `reports/tables/random_vs_future_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/precision_both_sets_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/challenge_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/leakage_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/gap_vs_tuned_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/automl_self_estimate_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/cv_comparison_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/drift_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/gap_adversarial_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/class_weight_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/calibration_quick.csv` and `eval_bias_facts.json`. Nothing here is typed by hand; the detail behind each section is in [`leakage.md`](leakage.md), [`temporal_bias.md`](temporal_bias.md) and [`explainability.md`](explainability.md), and the threats to validity are in [`limitations.md`](limitations.md).

**Machine.** Steps 2-3 (the tuning searches and the 60 s AutoML runs, and the saved models scored here) ran on a 16-core Intel Core Ultra 9 285H laptop (63 GB RAM, Windows) with 16 threads. Step 4 ran on a cloud VM: Intel(R) Xeon(R) Processor @ 2.10GHz, 4 logical CPUs, 15.7 GB RAM, Linux 6.18.44-fc-v185; the leakage runs used 4 threads (the part B and C fits 2, because those two jobs shared the machine). Every with/without comparison inside Step 4 was run on the VM with the same settings. No wall-clock time is compared across the two machines, and the AutoML scores at the Step 4 budget are not the Step 3 scores (fewer cores search fewer configurations in the same time).

**Test-set discipline.** No model, configuration, early-stopping point or threshold was chosen on test_random or test_future. The thresholds used below are 0.5, the 1%-FPR threshold set on TRAIN out-of-fold scores (`train_oof_1pct_fpr`) and the cost-sensitive deployment threshold in `models/threshold.json` (also set on TRAIN out-of-fold scores). CIs are 95%: bootstrap (1000 resamples, seed 42) or Wilson for rates.

## The answer in short

1. **Ranking metrics barely move for the tree models.** From test_random to test_future the tuned LightGBM goes from AUC 0.9924 to 0.9924 (gap -0.0001 [-0.0049, 0.0045]) and from TPR@1%FPR 90.4% to 90.0% (gap +0.43 pp [-6.50 pp, +6.46 pp]). The AUC gaps of all 7 tree models lie between -0.0001 and 0.0021; only LogReg, which cannot cap out-of-range feature values, loses much more (0.2184 AUC).
2. **The base rate matters more than the split.** At the threshold fixed on train (t = 0.9743) the false-positive rate falls from 0.80% on test_random to 0.60% on test_future (-25% relative). On the 50/50 test sets precision is 99.1% / 99.3%; at a realistic 1% malware prevalence it is 52.8% [35.8%, 69.3%] on test_random and 59.6% [39.8%, 76.6%] on test_future, i.e. 59 false alarms per 10,000 files scanned (random split: 79). Reading the 50/50 test_random as 1%-prevalence traffic changes precision by -46.27 pp; moving from test_random to test_future at that prevalence changes it by +6.72 pp: the 50/50 test mix is the larger source of optimism.
3. **The hard tail is invisible to both test sets.** On the 814 Win64 challenge-set files (malware that antivirus engines missed when first seen) the tuned model detects 19.4% [16.8%, 22.3%] at the same threshold, against 87.4% of ordinary test_future malware.
4. **No AutoML tool protects against the label leak.** With `detection_ratio` added, 4 of 4 models (tuned LightGBM and the three AutoML tools) ranked it first and scored test_future AUC 1.0000; 0 log lines named the column and 0 tools dropped it. The future split does not expose this leak either: a post-hoc label proxy is just as available (and perfect) in later weeks.
5. **AutoML does not protect against drift.** None of the 3 AutoML tools has a smaller AUC gap than the tuned LightGBM; none has a larger one (paired bootstrap CI of the gap difference), `reports/tables/gap_vs_tuned_quick.csv`. Every tool validated on a random split of `train` (the same weeks as its training rows): its own validation AUC was 0.9799 to 0.9921 on the clean runs, against test_future 0.9802 to 0.9923, and 1.0000 with the leak: a near-perfect score that no tool questioned.

From test_random to test_future: AUC +0.0001, TPR@1%FPR -0.43 pp, FPR at t -0.20 pp, precision at 1% prevalence +6.72 pp. From ordinary test_future malware to the challenge set: recall at t -67.98 pp.

* The two CV schemes bracket test_future AUC: stratified CV (every fold mixes all 52 weeks, like test_random) is optimistic, the forward CV (validate on later weeks only) pessimistic, partly because its folds fit on fewer rows (`temporal_bias.md`, section 2).
* AUC and TPR@1%FPR are per-class rates, so they do not depend on the 50/50 mix; precision does. That is why the base rate moves precision far more than the random-to-future step does.

## 3. Why the random-vs-future gap is small for trees (and not a bug)

* **The inputs do drift.** A classifier separates train files from future files with AUC 0.9082 (control, same weeks: 0.5148); the three time-stamp columns carry 36.9% of its gain (`reports/tables/gap_adversarial_quick.csv`, `reports/tables/gap_adversarial_top_features_quick.csv`). Trees cannot extrapolate: a value beyond the last split lands in the same leaf as the newest training values, which limits what drift in such columns can do to the detector.
* **Removing the time proxies inside X does not shrink the gap.** Without the COFF timestamp and the two signing-time columns the tuned model's AUC gap goes from -0.00009 to -0.00001 (`reports/tables/leakage_quick.csv`, `ablate_time_proxies`), so they are not a hidden time leak that flatters test_random.
* **No label shift.** The malicious share is 49.98% / 50.10% / 49.95% in train / test_random / test_future (`reports/tables/class_balance_quick.csv`), by construction, so the score cannot move through the class mix.
* **Short horizon, slow decay.** Over the 12 future weeks the weekly AUC slope is +0.00055 per week (`reports/tables/drift_quick.csv`); a model retrained on only the last 12 training weeks scores 0.9880 on test_future, against 0.9924 for the model trained on all 52: at this horizon, more data beats recency.
* **Weekly stratification** makes test_random an interpolation test: every test_random file has training files from its own week, which is the main reason it is the optimistic one.

* **`detection_ratio` is a label leak, and every tool took it.** It is the share of antivirus engines that flagged the file, i.e. the thing the label was made from. A column with AUC 1.0000 against the label (`reports/data_check_quick.json`) is exactly what a search for the best score is built to find, so no tool can be expected to reject it from the data alone; only a human question about provenance ('is this known when the file first arrives?') catches it.
* **`first_submission_date` is mostly harmless here, but for a fragile reason.** It changed test_future AUC beyond its CI for 0 of 4 models, by at most 0.0005. Every future date lies after the last training date, so for a tree the column is a constant on test_future; with a 50/50 class mix in every week it carries almost no label information. In data where the malware share changes over time, the same column would let a model learn the calendar.
* **The tools' own validation did not raise the alarm either:** with `detection_ratio` their internal validation AUC was 1.0000 (`reports/tables/automl_self_estimate_quick.csv`). A near-perfect AUC on malware detection should itself be treated as a bug report.

## 6. What protects, and what does not

* **A provenance review of every column, before training** (human): the only defence against `detection_ratio` in this project. It is cheap and no tool did it.
* **A temporal hold-out** (train on earlier weeks, test on later ones): it measures drift and makes a date column useless, but it cannot catch a post-hoc label proxy (section 4).
* **Thresholds and costs re-derived for the real base rate.** The 10:1 cost threshold in `models/threshold.json` (t = 0.0049), chosen on 50/50 train data, has FPR 12.99% on test_future and precision 7.1% at 1% prevalence (`reports/tables/prevalence_quick.csv`): fine on a balanced test set, costly in real traffic.
* **Class weights did not help:** 0 class-weighted refit(s) improved test_future AUC beyond the CI (`reports/tables/class_weight_quick.csv`). With 50/50 classes there is no imbalance to correct, and reweighting only shifts scores, which a threshold does as well.
* **Calibration fitted on train out-of-fold scores carries over to later weeks:** isotonic calibration changes the test_future Brier score by -0.0016 [-0.0035, -0.0000] (`reports/tables/calibration_quick.csv`). It makes the probabilities more trustworthy, not the ranking.
* **Retraining on recent weeks only did not help at this horizon:** 0.9880 vs 0.9924 test_future AUC (`reports/tables/drift_quick.csv`). The real remedy, retraining with labelled files from the deployment period, cannot be tested without using test_future for training.
* **A stress set of hard files** (the challenge set) is what showed the largest real-world weakness; neither random nor temporal splits of ordinary data show it.

## 7. Verdict

For a tree model on EMBER2024 Win64, a random split overstates the ranking quality only a little (AUC 0.9924 vs 0.9924 on later weeks). The bigger overstatement comes from reading a 50/50 test set as if it were real traffic: at 1% prevalence 59.6% of the tuned model's alarms on test_future are real, and it misses 81% of the malware that antivirus engines missed at first. The AutoML tools' Step 3 models reach test_future AUC 0.9892 to 0.9923 (tuned LightGBM 0.9924), and they lose no less to drift: none of the 3 AutoML tools has a smaller AUC gap than the tuned LightGBM; none has a larger one (paired bootstrap CI of the gap difference). They do not protect against leakage: given a post-hoc label column they all use it, report a near-perfect validation score, and say nothing. The protections that worked here were human ones: asking where each column comes from, testing on later data, and checking the operating point at a realistic base rate.
